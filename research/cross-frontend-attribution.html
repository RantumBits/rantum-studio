Cross-Frontend Attribution Methodologies in Decentralized Exchange Volume

Andrew Maury
• July 23, 2026 · v3, October 16, 2026 • DOI 10.5281/zenodo.21513262
PDF on Zenodo View on Zenodo

Abstract

Decentralized exchange (DEX) volume is used by liquidity-mining programs, Layer-2 (L2) foundations and grant committees as a proxy for user adoption. Once a trade settles in a shared liquidity pool, nothing in the protocol records which frontend (a wallet swap widget, an aggregator, an institutional contract, a meta-router) originated it, so volume figures cannot be tied to an origin without additional work.

This paper describes the attribution method used by ClearTrace on Ethereum, Base, Arbitrum and Optimism. Each transaction's volume is counted once and assigned to the contract the transaction entered through, to an aggregator, or (on Ethereum only) to an aggregator's integrating partner, and that contract is resolved to a name. Two further signals, an appended-calldata check and the recipients of fee transfers, are recorded alongside without adding to volume.

Over the seven days to 2026-10-12, ClearTrace attached a name to 86.9% of $13.00B in tracked DEX volume, but only 57.9% carried a name of a kind that identifies a protocol, organization or person, and about 40% did so on evidence other than the author's own inference or a labelling heuristic; 29.0% carried a descriptive label such as "Automated Trading Contract" or "Anonymous Vault Router", and 13.1% was untraced. Classified by ClearTrace's own name taxonomy, 18.6% of volume went to contracts named as user-facing frontends and 0.2% to in-wallet swap surfaces, while market makers, searchers, bots and anonymous contracts accounted for much of the rest. We also report a quote-deliverability study of six aggregators on Ethereum (2026-06-23 to 2026-07-02) and a reading of near-zero sandwich counts on Arbitrum. Attribution precision has not been measured against labelled ground truth (Section 6).
